# 02Ship Growth Strategy — Movement / Audience (A + B combined)

_Office-hours design doc — 2026-08-12_

## The diagnosis

- **Real asset:** 20-50 skills-motivated regulars in Sydney who would notice if 02Ship vanished. That is a genuine movement nucleus. Most communities with 700 signups have zero of this.
- **Reach (not demand):** 700+ registrations across 6 meetups is a mostly-dormant top-of-funnel list. Registration is the cheapest signal there is. Do not use it as your north star.
- **Core value / wedge:** members level up their Claude/AI building. This is defensible because it is local, in-person, live, and curated — things a YouTube tutorial cannot give.
- **Goal:** grow as a movement / audience. Monetization comes later, off reputation.

## Premises this plan rests on

1. Growth lever is **depth, not headcount** — 50 regulars into 200 who bring friends beats 700 into 1,500.
2. The movement currently **depends entirely on the founder** — it dies within ~2 months if you stop. A movement must outlive its founder.
3. The **skill transformation is invisible** — it happens in the room and disappears. Nobody outside Sydney sees a member go zero-to-shipped. That invisibility caps audience growth.

## The flywheel (what A + B actually is)

Member levels up -> ships something real -> demos it at the meetup -> demo becomes public content -> content teaches + attracts the next member AND gives the shipper status -> shipper becomes a speaker -> speaker becomes a co-organizer.

A feeds the flywheel with people and proof. B compounds it beyond the room. They are one loop, not two projects.

## Track A — Regulars flywheel (depth + distribute ownership)

1. **Name the skill ladder publicly.** Four visible stages: Newcomer -> Shipped -> Speaker -> Organizer. Recognize progression out loud at meetups. People climb ladders they can see.
2. **Institutionalize "ship & tell."** Every meetup: 3-4 members give a 5-minute demo of what they built. This is the heart of the whole thing — transformation made visible, and it is also your content pipeline.
3. **Recruit 2-3 co-organizers from the regulars now.** Give each real ownership: one owns content/recording, one owns newcomer onboarding, one owns venue/logistics. This is the single highest-leverage move for a founder-independent movement.
4. **Newcomer onboarding.** Assign a regular to greet and pair with first-timers, so the dormant 700 have a reason to become regulars. This is how reach converts into depth.

## Track B — Content engine (visibility + compounding audience)

1. **Record every demo.** Phone on a tripod is fine. Each 5-minute demo = one short video + one X thread + one LinkedIn post. Title pattern: "Sydney builder ships [X] with Claude in [Y] days."
2. **Weekly builder story.** Write up one member's zero-to-shipped journey. Feeds your daily news channel and rewards the shipper with status.
3. **Reflavor the daily AI news.** Add a "from the community" section so it is 02Ship-flavored, not generic. It becomes your top-of-funnel magnet.
4. **Monthly flagship artifact.** A compilation reel: "What Sydney built with Claude in [month]."

## Metrics that matter (track these, not registrations)

- **Repeat rate:** % of attendees who came to a prior meetup. Target: grow the regular core from ~30 to ~80 in 90 days.
- **Contributors:** distinct members who demoed or organized. Target: 15+ distinct demoers in 90 days.
- **Content shipped:** member ships documented publicly. Target: 20+ demo videos/threads.
- **Founder-dependency:** active co-organizers. Target: 3.
- **Explicitly NOT the goal:** raw registration count.

## Risks

- **A + B is more than one person can sustain.** That is exactly why co-organizers (A3) come first. If B's recording/posting load is not distributed, B quietly dies and you burn out.
- **Content disconnected from the room.** Keep B sourced from real member demos, not generic AI takes, or it becomes a hollow media channel.

## Opportunity (later, not now)

A skills-based Claude community in Sydney with visible member ships and a content trail is exactly what Anthropic developer relations wants to amplify or sponsor. Once Track B is producing artifacts, that is your credibility/sponsorship asset. Do not lead with it; earn it.

## The assignment (do this at or before your next meetup)

1. **Direct-ask 3 named regulars** to each own one thing: content/recording, newcomer onboarding, venue/logistics. Named people, not "who wants to help?" to the room.
2. **Lock "ship & tell" into the format:** 3 members demo 5 minutes each, and you record all of it.

Everything else in this plan follows from having co-owners and a demo pipeline.

# Execution Layer (CEO review — HOLD SCOPE)

_Added 2026-08-13. The strategy above (A + B combined) is accepted as-is. This layer makes it bulletproof: the failure modes that would actually kill it, the sequencing that prevents the main one, and the metrics that catch drift early._

## The dominant failure mode

**A + B is two jobs for one founder, and it fails silently.** The way this dies is not dramatic: Track B (content) quietly stops after ~3 weeks because you are doing everything, and nobody notices until the momentum is gone. Every other risk is downstream of this one.

The fix is sequencing, not parallelism. **Do not start Track B until a content owner exists.**

## Sequencing

```
Weeks 1-2   Track A foundation only: recruit 3 co-organizers, lock ship&tell,
            start capturing emails. No content work yet.
Weeks 3-6   Track B on top: the content owner turns each meetup's demos into
            clips + threads. You are not the one editing.
Weeks 7-12  Monthly compilation reel, weekly builder story, newcomer onboarding.
```

## Named failure modes (trigger -> what you see -> guard)

1. **Founder burnout.** You personally organize, record, edit, and post. -> You skip a meetup and everything stalls. -> Co-organizers own content ops before B launches. Alarm: you are still running more than one of {venue, content, onboarding} after week 4.
2. **Content treadmill decay.** No owner, editing is a chore. -> Videos stop, Track B is dead. -> Dead-simple pipeline: raw phone clip + auto-captions, no heavy edit. Alarm: 0 videos published for 2 weeks.
3. **Demos dry up** (fuel for both A and B). -> Nothing to record or show. -> Seed every meetup with your demo + 2 pre-committed regulars, book demos a week ahead, keep the bar low ("5 min, rough is fine"). Alarm: fewer than 3 demos booked before a meetup.
4. **Co-organizers don't stick.** Vague roles. -> They drift, it is all back on you. -> Named single-owner roles with real decisions and public credit. Target: 3 active at 90 days.
5. **Newcomers don't convert.** No onboarding, core feels cliquey. -> The 700 list stays dormant, the core never grows. -> Assigned greeter + a "your first ship" path. Metric: newcomer to 2nd-visit rate.

## Two cheap moves that keep bigger options open (do them now)

Building around A + B is the accepted call. These two cost almost nothing and de-risk the "rented platform" problem inside it:

- **Capture emails from day one.** Make the daily news email-subscribable. Telegram and Discord are rented land; an email list is the one piece of audience you actually own.
- **Tag which members actually shipped.** One line per demo. If you ever want an outcomes-based pitch (Anthropic, sponsors, paid cohorts) later, that record is the proof, and it is worthless if you start tracking it in month six instead of now.

## Metrics (review monthly)

The five above, plus two:

- **Bob-load index:** how many core ops you personally run. Target: trending toward 1.
- **Owned audience:** email list size (not Telegram/Discord counts).

## Verdict

A + B survives review **with one binding condition: sequence it (co-organizers before content), or the founder-burnout failure mode takes it out.** The assignment above holds, plus: start capturing emails this week.
